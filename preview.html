Course A · Retro games, old manga and toys

90s shonen and retro games

For Dragon Ball Z, Digimon, Cowboy Bebop, Mario and Zelda. We go through the 1990s shonen and game shelves: toys and figures, retro consoles, old manga, and a second-hand game shop for anything on your list.

The day

  1. 10:00
    Vie de France Akihabara DiningWe meet here, on the 1st floor of BiTO AKIBA. Radio Kaikan is a short walk away, so there is almost no walking to start. If you are tired, tell me and we slow down.
  2. 10:10
    Akihabara Radio KaikanTen floors facing the station. We take the lift to the top and walk down. Figures and toys are on 3F to 7F, and K-BOOKS is on 3F.
  3. 11:00
    Super Potato AkihabaraFamicom to PlayStation 2 on the 3rd and 4th floors, plus handhelds and Sega. The early Mario and Zelda games are on the Famicom and Super Famicom shelves. The 5th floor is a small retro arcade with a place to sit.
  4. 11:50
    Lunch at Go!Go!CurryKanazawa curry: a pork cutlet on a dark, thick roux, eaten at the counter. It is quick and close to the station.
  5. 12:40
    Mandarake Complex AkihabaraBoys' manga and vintage books on 3F. Toys on 6F to 8F, with Chogokin on 7F and robots on 8F. Stock changes every week, so we see what is there that day.
  6. 13:45
    Surugaya Akihabara Game-kanA second-hand game shop with deep stock: retro and current games, strategy guides and game magazines. Its website lets you search the shop, so we can look up a title from your list first. We finish here around 15:00.

The route

All on foot. Radio Kaikan is across the street from the station, and the rest of the day moves north toward Mandarake and Surugaya, with short walks between shops. Open the route in Google Maps ↗

Good for Fans of 1990s series and console games who like to dig through shelves.

Keep in mind Check that a Japanese game runs on your console at home, and on 100 V power, before you buy. A Famicom cartridge does not fit an NES without an adapter. Go!Go!Curry takes cash only, so bring some for the smaller shops too.

Radio Kaikan ↗ Super Potato ↗ Mandarake Complex ↗ Surugaya Game-kan ↗

Course B · Gunpla and model shops, with a retro stop

Gundam and models

For Gundam Wing. The morning and early afternoon are the model shops, with a stop at Super Potato and Radio Kaikan so the day is not only kits. You could leave with a Gunpla kit and a few tools, small enough to build in your hotel room and carry home.

The day

  1. 10:00
    Vie de France Akihabara DiningWe meet here, on the 1st floor of BiTO AKIBA. Yodobashi is a few minutes' walk away, so the first shop is close.
  2. 10:05
    Yodobashi AkibaPlastic models, modelling tools, figures and games are all on the 6th floor. We start with the current Gunpla kits and the tools you would need to build one. I can help you choose a kit, a Wing Gundam one if there is one in stock.
  3. 11:05
    Kotobukiya Akihabara-kanThe figure and model kit maker's own shop. It sells new releases and shop-only items, and the pop-ups change, so we see what is on that week.
  4. 11:50
    Super Potato AkihabaraA short stop so the day is not only models. Famicom to PlayStation 2 on the 3rd and 4th floors, and a small retro arcade on the 5th.
  5. 12:30
    Lunch at Go!Go!CurryKanazawa curry: a pork cutlet on a dark, thick roux, eaten at the counter. It is quick and close to the station. If you would rather sit down for a steak, Juke Steak serves lunch and I can swap it in.
  6. 13:20
    Volks Hobby Tengoku 2Seven floors of kits, figures and goods. Volks's own kits are on 4F, and the 6th and 7th floors host pop-up events that change.
  7. 14:25
    Akihabara Radio KaikanA short last stop, facing the station. Kaiyodo is on 5F, Yellow Submarine with its model kits on 6F, and Volks on 8F. We finish here at 15:00, right by the station.

The route

All on foot. We work from the station south to Kotobukiya and Super Potato, then north to Volks, and come back to Radio Kaikan by the station to finish. Open the route in Google Maps ↗

Good for Gunpla and model fans who want to build something while in Tokyo.

Keep in mind Go!Go!Curry takes cash only, so bring some for the smaller shops too. The Dragon Ball Store is not in Akihabara: it is on the 1st floor of Tokyo Station Ichibangai, two stops away, and I can add it if you want it.

Yodobashi Akiba ↗ Kotobukiya ↗ Volks Hobby Tengoku 2 ↗ Super Potato ↗ Radio Kaikan ↗

Course C · Akihabara in the morning, Nintendo and Pokémon after lunch

Akihabara and Shibuya: Nintendo and Pokémon

For Mario, Zelda and Pokémon. The morning is Akihabara for two stops, then one train to Shibuya, where Nintendo TOKYO and the Pokémon Center are on the same floor.

The day

  1. 10:00
    Vie de France Akihabara DiningWe meet here, on the 1st floor of BiTO AKIBA. Radio Kaikan is a short walk away.
  2. 10:10
    Akihabara Radio KaikanTen floors facing the station. We take the lift to the top and walk down. Figures and toys are on 3F to 7F.
  3. 11:00
    Super Potato AkihabaraFamicom to PlayStation 2 on the 3rd and 4th floors. The early Mario and Zelda games are on the Famicom and Super Famicom shelves. The 5th floor is a small retro arcade.
  4. 12:15
    Train from Akihabara to ShibuyaAbout 30 to 40 minutes by train. I ride with you and tell you which line to take.
  5. 12:55
    Lunch in Shibuya PARCOThe restaurants are on the 7th floor of the same building as the stores. We eat there, then go down one floor.
  6. 13:35
    Nintendo TOKYONintendo's own store on the 6th floor of Shibuya PARCO, five minutes on foot from Shibuya Station. On busy days entry is by numbered ticket, and a weekday is calmer. I check the store's page that morning.
  7. 14:20
    Pokémon Center ShibuyaThe official Pokémon store, on the same floor. We finish here at 15:00.

The route

Akihabara on foot in the morning, then one train ride of about 30 to 40 minutes. In Shibuya, lunch and both stores are in the same building, so there is almost no walking. Open the route in Google Maps ↗

Good for Nintendo and Pokémon fans who also want the Akihabara shelves, and do not mind one train ride.

Keep in mind Shibuya PARCO has irregular closing days, so I check its calendar the week before. New Pokémon card products are sold by advance lottery, so sealed packs are unlikely, but the store is worth it for everything else.

Nintendo TOKYO ↗ Pokémon Center Shibuya ↗ Shibuya PARCO ↗
