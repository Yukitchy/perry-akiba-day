Course A · Retro games, old manga and toys, close to the station

Akihabara core: 3 hours

For the first day after the flight. Three stops, short walks, and a place to sit on the top floor of Super Potato. It is built around the 1990s shonen and game shelves, which suits Dragon Ball Z, Digimon, Cowboy Bebop, Mario and Zelda.

The day

  1. 10:00
    Akihabara Station, Electric Town exitWe meet here. Radio Kaikan is across the street, so there is almost no walking to start. If you are tired, tell me and we slow down.
  2. 10:10
    Akihabara Radio KaikanTen floors facing the station. We take the lift to the top and walk down. Figures and toys are on 3F to 7F, and K-BOOKS is on 3F.
  3. 11:00
    Super Potato AkihabaraFamicom to PlayStation 2 on the 3rd and 4th floors, plus handhelds and Sega. The early Mario and Zelda games are on the Famicom and Super Famicom shelves. The 5th floor is a small retro arcade with a place to sit.
  4. 12:00
    Mandarake Complex AkihabaraBoys' manga and vintage books on 3F. Toys on 6F to 8F, with Chogokin on 7F and robots on 8F. Stock changes every week, so we see what is there that day. We finish here at 13:00.

The route

All on foot, about ten minutes at most between shops. Radio Kaikan is across the street from the station, and Mandarake is about four minutes from the Electric Town exit. Open the route in Google Maps ↗

Good for A slow first day: a few shops, one tall building of toys, and somewhere to sit.

Keep in mind Check that a Japanese game runs on your console at home, and on 100 V power, before you buy. A Famicom cartridge does not fit an NES without an adapter.

Course B · Gunpla and model shops, retro games, lunch and Mandarake

Akihabara deep: 5 hours

For the most of Akihabara in one day. It keeps everything in course A and adds the Gunpla and model shops, a sit-down lunch and a longer stay at Mandarake. Good if Gundam Wing is high on the list.

The day

  1. 10:00
    Akihabara Station, Electric Town exitWe meet here. Yodobashi is one minute away, so the first shop is almost at the door.
  2. 10:05
    Yodobashi AkibaPlastic models, modelling tools, figures and games are all on the 6th floor. A good first look at the current Gunpla kits.
  3. 10:55
    Akihabara Radio KaikanTen floors facing the station. Kaiyodo is on 5F, Yellow Submarine with its model kits on 6F, and Volks on 8F. We take the lift to the top and walk down.
  4. 11:45
    Super Potato AkihabaraFamicom to PlayStation 2 on the 3rd and 4th floors, plus handhelds and Sega. The 5th floor is a small retro arcade with a place to sit.
  5. 12:25
    Kotobukiya Akihabara-kanThe figure and model kit maker's own shop. It sells new releases and shop-only items, and the pop-ups change, so we see what is on that week.
  6. 12:55
    Lunch at Go!Go!CurryKanazawa curry: a pork cutlet on a dark, thick roux, eaten at the counter. It is quick and close to the station. If you would rather sit down for a steak, Juke Steak serves lunch and I can swap it in.
  7. 13:40
    Mandarake Complex AkihabaraBoys' manga and vintage books on 3F. Toys on 6F to 8F, with Chogokin on 7F and robots on 8F. Stock changes every week, so we see what is there that day.
  8. 14:30
    Volks Hobby Tengoku 2Seven floors of kits, figures and goods. Volks's own kits are on 4F, and the 6th and 7th floors host pop-up events that change. We finish here around 15:00.

The route

All on foot. Most legs are short, and the longest is from lunch to Mandarake. We work from the station south to Kotobukiya, then north. Open the route in Google Maps ↗

Good for Gunpla and figure fans who want the whole district in one day, with a proper lunch in the middle.

Keep in mind Go!Go!Curry takes cash only, so bring some for the smaller shops too. The Dragon Ball Store is not in Akihabara: it is on the 1st floor of Tokyo Station Ichibangai, two stops away, and I can add it if you want it.

Yodobashi Akiba ↗ Kotobukiya ↗ Volks Hobby Tengoku 2 ↗ Mandarake Complex ↗ Super Potato ↗

Course C · Akihabara in the morning, Nintendo and Pokémon in the afternoon

Akihabara and Shibuya: full day

For both worlds. The morning is the short version of course A, and the afternoon is Shibuya for Mario, Zelda and Pokémon, with Nintendo TOKYO and the Pokémon Center on one floor.

The day

  1. 10:00
    Akihabara Station, Electric Town exitWe meet here. Radio Kaikan is across the street.
  2. 10:10
    Akihabara Radio KaikanTen floors facing the station. We take the lift to the top and walk down. Figures and toys are on 3F to 7F.
  3. 11:00
    Super Potato AkihabaraFamicom to PlayStation 2 on the 3rd and 4th floors. The early Mario and Zelda games are on the Famicom and Super Famicom shelves. The 5th floor is a small retro arcade.
  4. 11:55
    Lunch at Go!Go!CurryKanazawa curry at the counter, quick and close to the station. An early lunch, so we are not hunting for food in Shibuya.
  5. 12:40
    Train from Akihabara to ShibuyaAbout 30 to 40 minutes by train. I ride with you and tell you which line to take.
  6. 13:30
    Nintendo TOKYONintendo's own store on the 6th floor of Shibuya PARCO, five minutes on foot from Shibuya Station. On busy days entry is by numbered ticket, and a weekday is calmer. I check the store's page that morning.
  7. 14:35
    Pokémon Center ShibuyaThe official Pokémon store, on the same floor. Thirty to forty-five minutes is enough for most people.
  8. 15:25
    Jump Shop ShibuyaOfficial Weekly Shonen Jump goods, also on the 6th floor. Dragon Ball is one of the series the Jump Shops cover, and what is in stock changes.
  9. 16:00
    Mandarake ShibuyaOne basement floor in the Shibuya BEAM building, five minutes from the Hachikō exit. Shonen comics, vintage comics and toys. We finish here around 17:00.

The route

Akihabara on foot in the morning, then one train ride of about 30 to 40 minutes. In Shibuya the three PARCO stores share one floor, and Mandarake is a five-minute walk away. Open the route in Google Maps ↗

Good for Nintendo and Pokémon fans who also want the Akihabara shelves, and do not mind one train ride.

Keep in mind Shibuya PARCO has irregular closing days, so I check its calendar the week before. New Pokémon card products are sold by advance lottery, so sealed packs are unlikely, but the store is worth it for everything else. A Gundam Base is announced for the 5th floor of the same building from November 2026, and I will check whether it has opened.

Nintendo TOKYO ↗ Pokémon Center Shibuya ↗ Shibuya PARCO ↗ Mandarake Shibuya ↗
